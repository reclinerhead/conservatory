# The web app

`web/` is a Next.js 16 App Router app on the Aviary's stack: React 19 with the React Compiler, TypeScript, Tailwind CSS 4, `node:sqlite` for the database, pnpm, Vitest. It serves every page a person sees and writes the rows their actions create. It never calls Pl@ntNet or a language model.

## Running it

```bash
cd web
pnpm install
pnpm dev
```

`allowedDevOrigins` in `next.config.ts` lets a phone on the house subnet reach the dev server. `turbopack.root` there is the repo root, not `web/`: `app/globals.css` imports `design/tokens.css` from outside `web/`, and Turbopack reads nothing outside its root (without it `pnpm dev` panics on every page; the build only gets away with it because Tailwind inlines the import). The same root bounds the build's file tracing, which is why `lib/db.ts` marks its runtime database path `turbopackIgnore` both where it is built and where it is checked. `pnpm test` runs Vitest once; `pnpm build` is the production build the boxes run with `pnpm start`.

## Reading the database

`lib/db.ts` opens `CONSERVATORY_DB` with `node:sqlite`'s `DatabaseSync`: `openReadOnly` for pages (returns `null` when the file does not exist yet, so a fresh checkout renders honest zeros instead of crashing) and `openReadWrite` for the routes that record a person's actions (sets a 5 s busy timeout because Fern writes to the same file, and foreign keys on). The environment is read with bracket access (`process.env["CONSERVATORY_DB"]`) on purpose: Next inlines a static `process.env.FOO` at build time, and these values belong to the box at runtime. Pages and routes that read the database export `dynamic = "force-dynamic"` so their answers come from the request, never from the build. `dataDir()` resolves `CONSERVATORY_DATA` the same way.

## Adding a plant

Both of the README's flows, the camera-roll batch after a trip and the photos taken in front of the plant, go through one page. The page sends one plant at a time, and a plant is all of its photos in one job, so Pl@ntNet sees the flower and the leaf together (its docs: up to five images of the same individual, and several "does improve the results, even if they are of the same type of view"). `components/uploader.tsx` is the client side:

- **The plant card** ("This plant", then "The next plant") is where a plant is put together: the two buttons, then five slots, always drawn and numbered, so a photo fills a slot and moves nothing; each photo has Remove until the plant is sent. Five is Fern's `MAX_IMAGES`. Choosing more than fit adds the first ones and says how many weren't; pressing either button with five already in says so instead of opening the picker, and a drop with five already in says the same.
- **Two inputs, and a drop.** "Take a photo" is a file input with `capture="environment"`; "Choose from the camera roll" takes `multiple` and names `.heic` and `.heif` in `accept`. On a computer, photos can also be dragged in from the desktop or a folder; "or drop photos here" beside the buttons says so only where the primary pointer is fine (Tailwind's `pointer-fine:`), so a phone never shows it. The whole window takes the drop, not just the card, because a drop that missed would make the browser open the photo in place of the page and lose the plants not yet sent. While files are over the page the card lifts (the hover border and shadow), the empty slots turn moss on sage, and the card's line says "Let go to add them to this plant."; nothing changes size. The listeners sit on `window` and act only on drags that carry `Files`. Enter and leave fire for every element crossed, so a count of them ends the lift; a mouse move, which never fires during a drag, clears one whose last leave never came. Nothing filters a drop the way `accept` filters a choice, so a dropped file that is neither an image by type nor a HEIC by name is left out and said ("1 file isn't a photo and wasn't added."). The previews are not draggable, so moving one about the page is never taken for a drop. Every way adds to the plant being put together.
- **No organ chips.** Every photo from this page goes as `auto`: Pl@ntNet detects the organ itself and its docs say to "submit images of different organs without worrying too much about it", and five chip rows do not fit under five slots on a phone. The confirm screen's "Add another photo" keeps its organ handling. In the chips' place, a tips aside under the card gives the three photos that help, from Pl@ntNet's ranking of the organs (flower, fruit, leaf, whole plant; bark least): the flower if it has one, a leaf filling the frame, the whole plant. There is no coin for scale, which the Quarry asks for: Pl@ntNet is a classifier with no notion of size and cannot read one.
- **"Send this plant"** moves the plant into the sent strip and leaves an empty next plant at once, and scrolls the page to the top (smoothly, or at once under reduced motion), because the button is at the foot of the card and the strip with Fern's status is above it. Nothing else scrolls the page: not Send again, not Remove, not an answer arriving. Plants send one after another through a promise chain, so the next can be put together while the last is still sending. The sent plants are one strip at the top of the page, in the dashboard's pending strip's shape (`SentStrip`): the latest three plants' own previews as overlapped 48 px thumbs (a leaf tile for one the browser can't draw), a heading, a line, and the button's room, always kept. Nothing is drawn there until the first plant is sent; it arrives on the person's own press, the one exception to the no-layout-shift rule, and after that its height never changes. What it says follows the most pressing state among the plants, in this order: a plant that couldn't be sent (only this page has its photos: "1 plant couldn't be sent", the reason in words, "Send it again" and "Remove", which retry or forget every failed plant together, since failures in one session share a cause); plants Fern has answered ("2 plants waiting for a name", the latest answer in the line: "Fern says *Ficus elastica* (rubber fig) for the last one, at 80%.", "Fern found no match for it; you can name it yourself.", or "Fern couldn't look at it; the name is up to you.", with "Name it" to `/name/[id]` for the first of them); plants still sending ("Sending photo 2 of 3, try 2 of 4…"); then plants with Fern ("In the queue…", "Fern is looking…"). The other plants follow in a sentence ("Besides it, 2 are with Fern."). "Back to the Conservatory", and the strip's line saying the page can be left, appear only once no plant is still sending or failed, because leaving loses those. Settled means identified, rejected, or failed for good, the rule every screen and query shares (below).
- **Shrink before sending** (`lib/shrink.ts`): `createImageBitmap` with orientation from EXIF, drawn to a canvas at most 1280 px on the long side, JPEG at 0.9. A 4 MB phone photo becomes about 300 KB, which is the difference between working and not on a weak Wi-Fi. A HEIC the browser cannot draw is decoded first, below; a file that can be neither drawn nor decoded is sent as it is and Fern re-encodes it.
- **A HEIC the browser cannot draw is decoded in the page** (#60). Most desktop browsers cannot draw HEIC, and every iPhone's camera roll is HEIC. The slot's `<img>` failing is the one sign of it (an iPhone draws HEIC itself and never gets here), so its `onError` starts `decodeHeic` (`lib/shrink.ts`): [heic-to](https://github.com/hoppergee/heic-to), libheif compiled to WebAssembly, through a dynamic `import()` inside the function, so it is its own chunk that only a browser which needs it fetches, once, and caches. Meanwhile the slot says "Reading the photo…" in the same place and size, the leaf mark pulsing (still under reduced motion); then the JPEG is the preview here and in the sent strip, and the send shrinks it like any other photo to a 1280 px JPEG. The decode is kept per photo as a promise in a ref (`decoded`), so a send during the decode waits for it and "Send it again" never decodes twice; a photo removed mid-decode is remembered in `gone` and left alone when its decode lands. `decodeHeic` returns null without loading the decoder for a file that isn't a HEIC by name or type, and null for one libheif rejects; then the slot shows "No preview" on a leaf tile and the original is sent. `shrinkToJpeg` itself tries the decode when the canvas path fails, so the plant page's Add a photo and the confirm screen's Add another photo send a decoded JPEG too, without a preview. The JPEG carries no EXIF, so the date is read from the original first, below.
- **The date survives the shrink** (`lib/exif.ts`): the re-encode drops EXIF, so `DateTimeOriginal` is read from the original bytes first, by a minimal APP1 walk, and sent as `takenAt`.
- **Photos first, then the job.** Each photo is its own `POST /api/photos` with retry (`lib/retry.ts`): four tries with waits doubling from 800 ms, each try cut off after 60 s so a request the Wi-Fi dropped mid-way cannot hang the plant. A 4xx other than 408 or 429 is `NoRetry`. Once every photo is in, `POST /api/jobs` with their ids makes the job, with the same retry. The `photos.id` each photo got is kept per photo, so "Send it again" sends only what hasn't gone, then makes the job. A 404 from `POST /api/jobs` (the photos were swept) forgets those ids, so the next try sends them afresh. Keys are a counter, not `crypto.randomUUID`, which a phone on the house's plain-http LAN does not have.
- **Then it polls.** Each sent plant's job is fetched from `/api/jobs/[id]` every 1.5 s (a round starts after the last one answered) until it settles, and the strip's heading changes in place. A job Fern will retry stays with Fern, and the strip's line says "Fern couldn't reach Pl@ntNet just now and will try again shortly." A round updates a plant when its job's status or `nextAttemptAt` changed, so a retry that becomes a final failure is seen.

**When a job is settled** (#61, `lib/job-state.ts`, tested). Fern marks a failed attempt `failed` with a `next_attempt_at` while it will still retry, and `failed` with none once it has given up (out of tries, or a 4xx; [fern.md](fern.md)). `isSettled` is the one rule: identified, rejected, or failed with no next attempt. `JobView` carries `nextAttemptAt` for it. `SETTLED_JOB_SQL` is the same rule in SQL, so a job Fern gave up on is waiting for a name like a rejected one: in `waitingJobIds` (the confirm screen's position, Next and `/name`), the dashboard strip's count and thumbs (`collectionCounts`, `waitingThumbs`), and named through the confirm route, which answers 409 for a job that is not settled. The module has no server imports, because the add page and the confirm screen are client components.
- **Nothing is kept in the browser.** The camera roll is the offline mode: a photo not yet sent when the page closes is still on the phone.

`POST /api/photos` opens the database first (503 while Fern has not created it, before anything is written). It writes the file to `photos/yyyy/mm/<uuid>.<ext>` under the data directory (dated by upload in UTC, extension from the MIME type, anything unknown stored as jpg for Fern to re-encode) and inserts a `photos` row with no job (`taken_at` when the EXIF had a date; `organ` from the form, `auto` when absent, which is what this page sends); a file whose row fails to go in is removed. Limits: 25 MB, image types only. Before storing, it deletes the photos that belong to neither a job nor a plant and are more than a day old, rows and files, the thumbnail Fern's idle sweep may have written included: what a plant that never got its job (the phone died, the page closed) leaves behind. A sweep that fails never fails the upload.

`POST /api/jobs` takes `{ photoIds }`: one to five distinct ids (`photoIdsFrom`). In one `BEGIN IMMEDIATE` it reads the photos' links and asks `planJob` (`lib/jobs.ts`, pure, tested) what to do: 404 when a photo is gone, 409 when one is already on a plant or the photos are split across jobs or some are on a job and some not, and when every photo is already on one job it answers that job, so a retry whose first response was lost gets the job it made rather than a second one. Otherwise it inserts the `jobs` row (`queued`) and points every photo at it, so Fern, which looks for queued jobs every second, never starts on half a plant.

`GET /media/...` serves originals and thumbnails with a year-long immutable cache behind `mediaRelPath`, a guard that accepts exactly the two shapes written into the data directory: `photos/yyyy/mm/<file>` from the upload route, and `reference/<species id>.jpg` or `.thumb.jpg` from Fern's enrichment pass. Photo names are UUIDs written once. A reference image can be rewritten in place by `python -m fern --enrich`, so `referenceUrls` adds the species' `enriched_at` as `?v=` and a re-fetched image is a new URL. A photo's thumbnail Fern has not written yet is answered with the original (`originalsForThumb` names the candidates), marked `no-store`, so a page shows the photo at once and the real thumbnail from the next load; anything else missing is a 404.

Tests: `lib/photos.test.ts` (paths, the media guard, the thumbnail fallback's candidates, a tile's two URLs, MIME mapping), `lib/exif.test.ts` (the APP1 walk against hand-built JPEGs, both byte orders, the IFD0 fallback), `lib/retry.test.ts`, and in `lib/jobs.test.ts` the job's body check and `planJob` (a plain make, a photo gone, one on a plant, all on one job, split across jobs, some on a job and some not).

## Naming a plant

`components/namer.tsx` is the confirm screen, one plant per screen, and it serves both flows: a person walking a strip of waiting photos, and one standing in front of the plant whose job has not settled yet (the screen polls `/api/jobs/[id]` every 1.5 s with the pill area reserved until Fern answers; while Fern will retry a failed job the reserved area says "Fern will try again shortly" instead of "Fern is looking…"). A job Fern gave up on is named like a rejected one: no pills, "Fern couldn't look at this one (its error). Type the name if you know it." and the name field. What is on it, and why:

- **The pills** come from `lib/pills.ts` (`planPills`, pure, tested on the three shapes from the Phase 0 run). Three rules: the top three species lead when the best score is confident; when the top three agree on a genus and the best species score is under 0.30, a **genus pill** leads ("Echeveria · species unsure", scored from the API's genus row or, failing that, the sum of the species scores); when the best score is under 0.10 and the genera disagree, the screen is in its **low-confidence state** and the name field comes first. Scores stay on every pill, never shown as 0%.
- **One tap names it.** A pill, the "Name it" button, a roster chip, or "Same plant as the last one" all call `POST /api/jobs/[id]/confirm` and the save is immediate: there is no batch save. The route finds or creates the species (looked up by hand, case-insensitively, because `UNIQUE(scientific_name, cultivar)` does not catch two NULL cultivars), creates the specimen, links the job's photos and writes the `confirmations` row in one transaction. A species named from a candidate, a pill or a typed name that matches one, takes the candidate's first common name (`firstCommonName` in `lib/jobs.ts`, tested) when it has none yet, so the name on the pill is the name on the tile (#62); Fern's enrichment fills it from GBIF only when it is still empty. The optional **nursery-tag field** becomes the species' `cultivar`.
- **The name field** autocompletes over `GET /api/species`, the roster match: species the household already owns, most-owned first, so the second pot of anything is one tap. A typed name the collection does not know becomes a species row with the typed text as `scientific_name` and no GBIF id; the enrichment pass (Phase 2) resolves it.
- **Change** (`DELETE .../confirm`) reverses a naming cleanly: the confirmation goes, the photos unlink, the specimen is deleted if nothing else is attached to it and the species if no specimen is left, and the job is back in the strip. The pills were never discarded, so they reappear as they were. A job whose photos were all removed from the plant's page answers 409, since the strip would get a job with nothing to show.
- **Skip for now** is a link to the next waiting job; the skipped one stays in the strip. **Not a plant** is two taps on purpose (mis-taps are common) and then deletes the job, its photos and their files. **Add another photo of this plant** stores a second photo on the same job and sends it back to the queue; the old pills stay until the new ones arrive.
- **Next** goes to the next waiting job, computed server-side (`nextWaitingJob` in `lib/jobs.ts`, tested): the next later job, wrapping to the first, or the Conservatory when nothing else waits.
- **The first glossary marks** (#10) are here: confidence, scientific name, genus (with the plant's own genus as the example), species, cultivar.

## The collection

The life list on the home page is a grid of species, `components/species-grid.tsx` over the pure shape in `lib/species-grid.ts` (tested). A person opens the app to learn about a plant, and the species page is where the care sheet, the summary and the relatives live, so a tile opens the species and the species page's In your collection leads on to each plant. The server sends every species as a row (`speciesList` in `lib/species.ts`: names, genus and family, the living count, the living plants counted by place as a JSON array built in the same query, the count of plants gifted or died, when its newest plant was added, and the thumbnail); the client filters and sorts on the phone, so a search makes no request.

- **Photos are squares** (#83), here and on every page: each box that crops a photo is `aspect-square`, its width set by its grid column, and so is the tinted or dashed tile that holds a photo's place. Most of the household's photos are portraits from a phone; the fixed-height landscape boxes the mockups draw kept about a third of one, and a square keeps three quarters of a portrait and of a landscape alike. The stored files are not cropped (Fern keeps each photo's own proportions), so the crop is CSS alone and the lightbox still shows the photo whole.
- **The tile** is the specimen card's recipe: the species' thumbnail in a square (the species photo, which leads the species gallery, else the reference, else a tinted tile, the five greens varied between neighbours). The grid is two across on a phone, three from 640 px and four from 1280 px (at 1024 px the side column leaves the list too narrow for four), so a square tile is never a screen tall. Fern's thumbnail is 320 px on its long side, so a square shows 240 px of a portrait or a landscape: too small for a phone at 3× or a wide tile at 2×. So the row carries the photo's 1280 px file as well (`tileUrls` in `lib/photos.ts`: `thumbUrl` and `imageUrl`) and the `<img>` offers both through `srcset`, the thumbnail declared at its honest `240w`, with a `sizes` that follows the grid's columns; a 1× desktop keeps the thumbnail, a phone takes the full file. Every photo tile wider than a thumb does the same: Get to know, the specimen card (`Tile.imageUrl`), the relative card, and the gallery tiles of the species and plant pages. Small thumbs (48 and 56 px) use the thumbnail alone. Under the photo, the common name in bold with the italic binomial under it, or the binomial alone in italics with that line reserved, so the tiles of a row are the same height. Then the meta line for the chip on screen (`tileLine`): at All, "3 plants · 2 places", the place's name when every living plant is in one place, or the count alone when none has a location; at a place or at "No location yet", "1 plant here" or "2 of 3 plants here"; at "No longer here", "1 plant no longer here". It says plants, not specimens, because a tile is one link and cannot carry the specimen info mark. The whole tile is one link to `/species/[id]`.
- **The filter row** (`placeChips`): "All" with every species that has a living plant, each place A to Z with the species that have a living plant there, then "No location yet" and "No longer here", each only when someone is in it. A species in two places counts under both; a species whose plants are all gifted or died is only under "No longer here". Counts come from the whole list, so the row never changes shape while a person types. Places match exactly as the plant pages write them, so "Shelf A" and "shelf A" are two chips, as they are on the tags page.
- **Sort by**, in the life list's header (`SortSelect`): Name, A to Z (the default, by the name the tile shows, then id) or Latest added (by when each species' newest plant was added, any status, newest first, then by name), applied by `gridRows` under every chip and search. The choice belongs to the device, not the view, so it lives in `localStorage` (`conservatory.sort`), not the URL, and survives a reload and a new session. The select and the grid read it through one small store with `useSyncExternalStore`, so they agree without a shared parent. The server draws Name, and a stored Latest added takes over on hydration. Every access is guarded, and a blocked storage keeps the choice in memory for the visit; a stored value not on the list reads as Name (`readSort`). The control is the Quarry's: a native `<select>` (the `.select` recipe), a drawn chevron, and every label invisible in the same grid cell so choosing never changes its width.
- **The search** (`GridSearch`) sits in the life list's header beside Sort by, and is the only search box in the app. It matches any part of the common name, the scientific name with cultivar, GBIF's spelling, the genus or the family (`matchesSpecies`), ignoring case. Typing a family name is the family filter. It filters as you type, and a link that lands on `/?q=` (a breadcrumb's family or genus, a What you grow most row) shows its query in the box. Search and chip combine. On a phone the box and the select share one row, the select without its visible label, and they and the place chips are 36 px tall with 13 px text (utilities at the call site; the `chip-compact` and `select` recipes keep 40 px for every other screen).
- **Both live in the URL** (`?q=` and `?at=`), written with the native `history.replaceState`, which Next syncs with `useSearchParams`: the back button returns to the same view, the search box and the grid agree, and nothing is fetched. An `?at=` that names no chip falls back to All.
- **No match** draws the "nothing" card in the tile's shape with "Show every species", which clears both, beside two placeholder tiles drawn at a tile's exact height (`BlankTile`). "Showing 3 of 12 species" is always there, counted against the chip on screen. An empty collection draws "No species yet" with "Add the first one" in the same shape.
- **Pot tags** are printed from the Tags pill in the top bar, which opens `/tags` with every plant; the tags page has its own place chips, with the same keys as the grid's.

Specimen cards (`SpecimenCard` in `components/collection.tsx`, on the species page and nowhere else now) are DESIGN.md §4's: photo or tinted tile (one of the five tile greens, varied between neighbours) in a square, two across on a phone, three from 640 px and four from 1280 px, then the names (`cardNames`): the common name (capitalised by `displayName`) with the italic species under it, "India Rubber Fig / *Ficus elastica 'Ruby'*", else the species alone. A plant has no name of its own: every plant of a species goes by the species' name, here, on its page, on its pot label and in the species gallery. One line under the name is always reserved, so when Fern finds a common name the text changes, not the height, and the meta lines line up across a row; the placeholder cards are drawn at the same height. Then "Location · since May 2019" (or "added Oct 2026" when no acquired date is known), and a tag for a plant no longer here. The whole card is one link to `/plants/[id]`. The species page's compact cards leave the common name out, since every card there is that species and its common name is the page's title; with no second line ever to fill, they reserve none, and the meta line sits directly under the name.

## A specimen

`app/plants/[id]/page.tsx` is one plant, read by `readSpecimen` in `lib/specimens.ts`: breadcrumb, the title block, tags for status, place and photo count, then the sections below. The title block is named the way its dashboard card is, by `cardNames`, and drawn the way the species page's is: the title is the common name, with the italic binomial (cultivar upright in quotes) under it; with no common name the binomial is the title and the line under it says "No common name on file". So a page reads "Tree Aeonium / *Aeonium arboreum*" or "*Ficus elastica* / No common name on file". The scientific-name info mark ends that line. The line is always drawn, so Fern finding a common name changes the text but not the height. The lightbox's dialog is named with the same name. The breadcrumb is Collection › the species › "Plant #142": the species crumb uses the same name, italic only when it is the binomial, and links to the species page, and the plant goes by its id, the number on its pot tag, since it shares the species' name. The tab title uses the species' name. The sections:

- **Photos as a timeline**, main photo first, then newest first by `taken_at` (upload time when the EXIF had no date). The choice counts only while the photo is on a plant of this species: `speciesGallery` ignores an id that is not among the species' photos and `speciesList`'s SQL joins through `specimens` for the same test, so the page and the thumbnail agree. ## The lightbox

## The to-do list

A to-do (#103) is the owner's own copy of one finding from a health check: what Fern saw, the remedy and likely or possible are copied into a `todos` row for that plant ([fern.md](fern.md), v12) when the finding is added. The copy is what every screen shows, so a to-do outlives its check: `DELETE /api/plants/[id]/health/[checkId]` clears the link (`ON DELETE SET NULL`) and the to-do stays. It goes with its plant. These are not the reminders of #42, which are worked out from the care rhythm on each request and never stored.

- **`POST /api/todos`** reads the finding from the stored answer by its place among the findings the Health card draws (`findingAt`, which goes through `healthView`, so the place the page sends is the place the server reads); the page never supplies the text. A unique index on the check and the place makes one finding one to-do, and the insert is `OR IGNORE`, so two tabs adding the same finding both get the one row.
- **`/todo`** (`app/todo/page.tsx`, `components/todo-list.tsx`): the title, a counts tag ("2 to do · 5 done"), then two columns in the dashboard's split. **To do**, in the main column, is the pending to-dos newest first; a row is a 44 px checkbox target with a 28 px box, the plant's 56 px thumb (its main photo) and name (`cardNames`, both opening the plant), the remedy clamped to two lines, and a line saying when it was added. The remedy is a button that opens the row in place: what Fern saw, the likely or possible tag, "Open this plant", a link to its species page, and "Remove". On a phone the remedy runs the row's full width under the checkbox, photo and name. **Done**, in the side column, is the done to-dos, the one done last first, as side-panel rows with the same checkbox ticked; ten, then "Show all". A to-do on a plant that is gifted or dead carries the plant's status tag. Both sections are always drawn; an empty one says so on a reserved row.
- **Nothing moves under the pointer.** The component keeps the two lists it was loaded with and lays each press over them: ticking strikes the remedy through and says "Done today. Untick it to put it back.", unticking in Done says "Back on your list", and removing leaves a line in the row's place saying where to add it again. A row changes list on the next load. Each press is sent at once and put back, with the server's words on the row's reserved line, if it fails; it also calls `router.refresh()`, which the lists ignore but the top bar's count does not.
- **The top bar's count** is read in the root layout (`pendingTodoCount`), which awaits `connection()` so the synchronous query runs on every request and is never baked into a page that would otherwise be static.
- **The dashboard's To do card** leads the side column: "All (N)" to `/todo`, then the three newest pending (`latestPending`) as side-panel rows, each opening `/todo`: the thumb at the top of the row, the plant name on one line, and the remedy wrapped and clamped at five lines (`line-clamp-5`, about three sentences at the column's width; most remedies fit whole). While any are pending the card wears DESIGN.md's highlighted-card pair, the `warn-bg` fill and `warn-line` border, with `warn` dividers, so it reads first; amber there means "waiting on you". With none pending it is a plain card whose one row says so. The card is as tall as its rows, one to three, each as tall as its remedy, with no blank rows. It is the one side card that does not hold its full height, since the owner reads blank rows here as missing to-dos and a long to-do is meant to nag; it sits at the top of the column, so its height moves only the cards below it, and only between page loads. Because the remedy wraps, it no longer sets the side column's width, which stays the same whether anything is pending or not.

Tests: `lib/todos.test.ts` covers the three routes' body and id checks (the text is never taken from the page), the finding at a place (among the findings the page draws, past the end, a check not done or unreadable), each finding's state for the Health card, the two lists' orders, the dashboard's three, and the counts line. The routes and the components are checked on a scratch database with a real browser.

## Pot tags

Every plant gets a QR label for its pot that opens its page from the phone's camera (#36). The pure parts are `lib/tags.ts` (tested), the code is drawn by `lib/tag-code.ts` on the server, the print page is `app/tags/page.tsx` over `components/tag-printer.tsx`, and the sheet is the `label-sheet` recipe in `globals.css`.

- **The address.** A tag encodes `<CONSERVATORY_URL>/t/<specimen id>` (`tagUrl`). `/t/[id]` sends a plant still in the collection, living or gone, to `/plants/[id]` with `redirect()`, an HTTP 307: temporary, because a phone caches a 308 and what a scan opens is meant to change later (a last-watered page, #3's cutting flow) without reprinting a label. The segment has no `loading.tsx`, so the redirect is never streamed as a meta tag. A removed plant's tag gets a small "no longer in the collection" page, and since specimen ids are never reused (schema v5, [fern.md](fern.md)) a tag cannot open the wrong plant. The short path keeps the code at version 3 (29 × 29 modules) at error-correction level Q. Rejected: encoding `/plants/[id]` (what a scan opens could never change), and a separate tags table with codes of its own (no sheet could be drawn without first writing rows, and the label's number would not be the plant's).
- **`CONSERVATORY_URL`** is read at runtime with bracket access (`tagBase`) and never taken from the request, since a sheet printed from the dev server would otherwise put a dev address on labels that last years. Unset, or not http(s), no code is drawn anywhere: the label's code square and the cards' 84 px tile are dashed placeholders that say why, Print is off with a line saying what to set, and nothing moves. On orchid it is `https://conservatory.lan` ([ops.md](ops.md)); at the desk, the dev server's LAN address, so a phone can scan a test print. `/tags` shows the host the codes point at.
- **Drawing a code.** `qrcode`'s `create()` at level Q, on the server only; `codePath` turns the module matrix into one SVG path, a rectangle per horizontal run, drawn in JSX by `TagCodeSvg` (`components/tag-code.tsx`) in pure black rather than `ink`, because a mono laser prints a near-black as a dot screen and blurs the module edges. No `dangerouslySetInnerHTML` and no QR library in the client bundle: `/tags` sends each row's path with it. The client imports `lib/tags.ts`, so that file must never import `qrcode`.
- **The stock** is the 1″ × 2⅝″, 30-up address label in the Avery 5160 layout (½″ top margin, 3⁄16″ sides, 3 across at a 2¾″ pitch, 10 down at 1″), which its weatherproof versions share. `label-sheet` writes it once in `em`, with 1em one inch of paper: on screen the sheet's font-size is its frame's width over 8.5 (`.label-sheets` is a size container, `100cqw / 8.5`), so the preview is the printed sheet's own markup scaled to fit; in print it is `1in`. A label is the code at 0.78″ with 0.11″ of the label's white round it as its quiet zone, then three one-line, ellipsised lines from the card's naming rule (`cardNames`): the name, the italic species, and "Shelf 2 · #142" (or "#142"), so a label means something without a phone. The paper's sizes and its black on white are the one place `globals.css` picks values outside the tokens.
- **Printing.** `@page { size: letter; margin: 0 }`, and in print only the sheets: anything that neither holds `.label-sheets` nor sits inside it is `display: none` (by `:has()`), and what holds it loses its margins, padding and borders, so sheet one starts at the top left corner of page one. Each sheet breaks after itself but the last. Label outlines, used labels and the sheet captions are screen only. The page says to print at 100%, not "fit to page", with margins none, and to try plain paper against a sheet first. Rejected: a PDF made on the server (exact without print settings, but a dependency and a download step; revisit if a printer disagrees).
- **Choosing.** `/tags` lists every living plant, one row each (a 44 px checkbox, the 56 px thumbnail, the name and species, "Shelf 2 · #142", and "Tagged <date>" or "No tag yet"), grouped by place as the collection is. The collection's location chips (same grouping, same `?at=` values) and a "Not tagged yet" toggle (`?untagged=1`) narrow it, and `?species=` or `?plant=` narrows it to one species or one plant, with "Show every plant" to clear. All of it runs on the client over the rows the server sent, kept in the URL with `history.replaceState`. On arrival the untagged plants on screen are checked; a plant asked for with `?plant=` ("Print this tag") is checked whatever its state. What prints is what is checked and on screen, in the list's order.
- **Start at label N** (`sheetSlots`, `nextStart`), for a partly used sheet: a 3 × 10 map of 44 px cells; tapping the first free label marks the ones before it as used (struck through) and shades the ones this print fills. Labels beyond the first sheet flow onto further sheets, each from its label 1. After Mark as tagged the page keeps where the print stopped in `localStorage` (`conservatory.tags.nextStart`, per browser, every access in try/catch) and offers it as the next start; it is read through `useSyncExternalStore`, so the server render starts at label 1 and nothing else moves when the browser's value arrives.
- **Mark as tagged.** The page cannot tell a printed sheet from a cancelled dialog (`afterprint` fires for both, Ctrl+P included), so when the dialog closes a strip asks "Did they print? Mark these 12 as tagged." Its space is always kept. `POST /api/tags` with `{ ids }` (1 to 600 positive integers) stamps `tagged_at` on those still living in one `UPDATE ... json_each` and returns the count; the page then refreshes, and the specimen card, the species card's count and "Not tagged yet" all read the same column. Rejected: marking when the sheet renders or on `afterprint`.
- **Before v5** the column does not exist: `hasTaggedAt` checks for it, so every page renders against a database Fern has not migrated yet with every plant untagged, and the mark route answers 503.

Not built: a scan page that says when the plant was last watered (needs the event log), the cutting-of-this-plant flow (#3), other label stocks, a printer offset nudge, and a scanner inside the app (the phone camera already does it).

Tests: `lib/tags.test.ts` covers `tagUrl` (trailing slashes; unset and non-http(s) bases), `codePath` (a hand-made matrix, every dark module covered and no light one), `sheetSlots` (no sheet for no tags, the used labels before the start, flowing onto further sheets, the last sheet's count, the clamp to 1..30) and `nextStart` (wrapping after label 30). The routes, the redirect and the components are checked by hand.

## The glossary

`lib/glossary.ts` holds every term the app explains (scientific name, genus, species, family, cultivar, specimen, confidence, common name, organ, and from the species page taxonomy, kingdom, phylum, class, order, author, and the care sheet's light, watering, soil, hardiness zone, fertilizing and dormancy, and the plant page's health check), keyed by slug, two or three plain sentences each with a "more" link. `components/term.tsx` is the info mark from DESIGN.md §4: an 18 px ring after the term, an "i" in moss, and on tap a sage note that explains it. The note floats under the mark rather than expanding in place, so opening one never moves anything: the no-layout-shift rule outranks the mockup's inline block. A note that would cross the window's right edge slides left until it fits 16 px inside it (measured before it paints), so a mark near the side column's edge never widens the page. An optional `example` lets a screen add a sentence about the person's own plants. `lib/glossary.test.ts` scans every `.tsx` for `<Term k="...">` and fails on a key the glossary lacks, and keeps every entry to a short paragraph. A key passed as a value rather than a literal (the care cells' `CARE_CELLS[].term`) is typed `GlossaryKey`, so the compiler checks it instead.

## The visual language

Defined in [`design/DESIGN.md`](../../design/DESIGN.md), made with Claude Design before any screen was built: pale sage ground, white cards, one moss action colour with pink, amber and blue reserved for bloom, time-sensitive and water, pills for everything pressable, Fraunces for display and Nunito Sans for reading. Read it before touching UI; the two static mockups in `design/mockups/` are the reference for the dashboard and species pages. How the app wires it:

- **Tokens.** `design/tokens.css` is the Tailwind 4 `@theme`, imported from `app/globals.css`. Nothing else in `web/` defines a colour, a face, a radius or a size: a screen says `bg-sage`, `text-ink-2`, `rounded-card`, `text-h2`, `max-w-page`.
- **Fonts** are self-hosted through `next/font` in `app/layout.tsx` (Fraunces with its italic for binomials, Nunito Sans at 400, 600, 700); the variables they set feed the two font tokens, so the boxes make no font requests. The Google Fonts `@import` that DESIGN.md's tokens originally carried was removed for this reason.
- **Component recipes** (DESIGN.md §4) are written once in `globals.css` under `@layer components`: `btn` with `btn-primary`, `btn-soft`, `btn-ghost`, `btn-compact`; `chip` with `chip-on`, `chip-compact`; `card` with `card-link` for the hover lift; `tag` with `tag-bloom`, `tag-warn`, `tag-water`; `nav-pill`; `thumb`; `sp` for an italic Latin name; `field` for a text input in the chip's pill.
- **The top bar** (`components/topbar.tsx`) is in the root layout: leaf mark and wordmark, a nav pill for each page that exists (Collection, lit on `/`, on a species page and on a plant's page; Tags, lit on `/tags`; To-do, lit on `/todo`, followed by the number still to do in the chips' count style, zero included, #103), and the primary "Add a plant" on the right. Below 640 px the pills are a size down (13 px, 8 px side padding) and never wrap, so the three and the button fit the one row at 360 px. It has no search box and is one row at every width: below 640 px the wordmark's text is for screen readers only, so the leaf is the home link, and the button reads "Add". Locations, Reminders and Flower Show get a pill when they get a page.
- **The dashboard** (`app/page.tsx`) follows DESIGN.md §5 in its Phase 1 subset: the pending strip (a sage card with three overlapping thumbs, real ones when photos are waiting, the headline, a detail line and one primary button; below 640 px it is a single line about 54 px tall with one thumb, the headline and a small "Name them" when something waits, the same height in both states), the life list with its counts line, location chips and 4-across species tiles (2 below 640 px, 3 below 1280), and the side column's five cards, in this order: To do (#103, see The to-do list), New species added recently, Get to know, Collection at a glance, What you grow most. **New species added recently** lists the four species that joined the collection last (`recentSpecies` in `lib/dashboard.ts`): newest first by `species.created_at`, when the name was first confirmed, ties to the newer id, only species with a living plant. A new pot of a species already owned does not bring it back; new plants are the grid's Latest added sort. A row is one link: the `thumb` with the tile's thumbnail rule, the tile's name (common name, else the italic binomial), and the binomial with the day it joined. The card is a filter over the grid's rows (`speciesList` carries `addedAt`), so it makes no query of its own. It is always four rows tall: blank rows of sage bars hold the rest, and with no species the first row says the first plant named shows up there. It has no "All N species" foot link, since the grid it would open is beside it and the sort lives in the browser, not the URL. **Your collection at a glance** has four tiles, specimens · species · families · locations, the first three with their info marks. The families tile is the length of What you grow most's tally, so the two never disagree. The waiting count is the pending strip's alone. **What you grow most** (`familyTally`) groups the species with a living plant by family, matched as the relatives block matches them (`taxonKey` in `lib/relatives.ts`, case and stray spaces ignored), and orders the families by how many species they hold, then by name; a family is shown by the spelling most of its species carry. The card is always five rows tall: a row is the family, "N species" (species, not plants, so it matches the tiles the row opens), a moss bar on a sage track measured against the top family in twelfths (`barTwelfths`, a fixed set of width classes rather than an inline style), and the genera owned in it, A to Z, three and "+N more" (`generaShown`). Each opens `/?q=<family>`, which the search matches. Blank rows of sage bars hold the rest; with no family placed the first says so. A foot line counts the families in all and any living species GBIF has not placed. **Get to know** (`spotlightPick`) shows one species a day: of those with a living plant and a stored summary (`speciesList` carries `hasSummary`), in a fixed shuffled order (a hash of the id), the one at the station's local day number (`localDay`) modulo their count. The pick holds all day, turns at local midnight, and every eligible species has its day before any repeats; a new species can change the day's pick. The page reads that one species whole (`readSpecies`) and draws its thumbnail in a square the card's width, its names, the tile's meta line, the first paragraph of its summary with its Latin names in italic (`latinRuns`), clamped to four lines in a four-line box, the care sheet's Light and Water headlines as tags with their info marks (sage skeleton tags before a sheet exists), and "More about …" to its page. Where the card is wide (the side column drops under the list below about 950 px, so on a tablet the card is the page's width), a container query puts the square beside the text at 208 px instead, so the photo is never a screen tall. With no eligible species, an invisible copy of those lines sets the card's height and the message sits over it. Every side card but To do keeps its height in every state. The counts line and the tiles count living plants; the "All" chip counts every card, the gone ones included. Greeting, Today's care, the other filters, Blooming now and the Flower Show wait for the phases that have data for them; their space is not faked.

### Rules every screen keeps

- **No layout shift.** Empty states reserve the populated space. The exception is a phone's single column, where space reserved to line cells up across a row has nothing to line up with and reads as a gap: the care cells and the empty relatives cell reserve their height from 640 px only. The pending strip keeps its height in every state and lays out its button even when hidden; the life list's empty state is drawn in the specimen card's own shape. Counts render on the server.
- **Honest zeros.** The counts say "0 specimens" rather than hiding.
- **Phone first, laptop wide.** Rows wrap, the side column is a flex sibling that drops below on a phone, card grids step down by width, chip rows wrap and never scroll sideways.
- **Tap targets** are at least 44 px tall, the buttons and chips 44 by recipe.
- **The app teaches** (#10). Every horticultural term carries an info mark; the glossary component lands with the confirm screen.
